A gift for a big birthday

For the birthday of the person
who remembers the most.

In every family there is one person who still holds the whole thing. Who was married to whom, which street the house was on, what the town was called before they changed the name. She is usually the oldest one at the table, and everybody assumes there is still time to ask her properly.

A Family Story Map puts her life back on the map of the world she actually crossed. Every town she lived in, her own photographs where they belong, her own words beside each one. Tap her name and her road draws itself, from the house she was born in to the country she ended in.

Her grandchildren read it in English, her sister reads it in Russian, the cousins who ended up somewhere else read it in whatever language they kept, all from one link, on the same evening. And it is the rare present a whole family can give together.

The night before you decide

She will say she does not need anything. She will mean it.

You have already been down the list. Another scarf. A voucher for a restaurant she will not want to sit in for three hours. Jewellery, which she will thank you for warmly and put in the drawer, next to the last one.

It is the same problem whether it is an eightieth birthday, a ninetieth, a golden wedding anniversary or the first big family gathering in years. The trouble is not that she is hard to buy for. The trouble is that at eighty the things she still wants are not sold anywhere. She wants to be asked. She wants to know that somebody has written it down. She wants to be sure that when she is no longer at the table, everybody still sitting round it will know where they came from.

That is what this present is. Not an object. The family sitting down and saying: your life is worth mapping, and we did not want to leave it any longer.

What they open

A century of her life, in the language each of them reads.

One link opens in every language your family reads, and each reader picks their own. Stories, names, tree and every button switch together. Shown with the family’s permission.

Open a place and the story is waiting. The archive photograph, who in the family is connected to it, the passage in the writer’s own words, and a line on what stands there today. Nothing paraphrased, nothing invented.

And it prints as a book. Any selection of places exports to a PDF: the photograph, the story in the family’s own words, and a QR code that opens the location. Print it, bind it and wrap it, so there is something in her hands on the day.

What actually happens on the day

The present is not the map. It is the two hours after it opens.

Somebody opens it on the biggest screen in the house. The journey draws itself across the map, and the first place opens: the town she was born in, a photograph, a few lines in her own words.

She bursts into tears. Nobody expected that, and for a moment nobody knows what to do, and it turns out to be the best thing that happens all day.

Then she starts talking. She tells you the story behind a photograph you have looked at your whole life without ever knowing what it was. She remembers a detail that is written down nowhere, and that she has not thought about in fifty years, and she remembers it only because she is looking at the street where it happened. She corrects you, twice.

That is the present. Everyone quiet, her talking, the grandchildren hearing it for the first time in their lives, and one of them holding up a phone to record, because it has finally occurred to somebody that this should be recorded.

Questions we are asked

About giving this as a gift.

What do you give a grandmother who says she wants nothing?

Something that is not an object. A Family Story Map is her own life on one interactive map: every town she lived in, her photographs where they belong, and her own words beside each place. It arrives as a link the whole family opens together, and as a printed book she can hold on the day.

How long does it take, and can it be ready for the birthday?

Tell us the date in your first message and we will answer honestly. A full family map is commissioned work and takes weeks rather than days. If the birthday is sooner than that, First Chapter, five researched places for $195, is ready in a week and can be given on the day, with the full map following after. The $195 comes off the price of the full map.

Can several people share the cost?

Yes, and most families do. Children, grandchildren and relatives abroad each put in what they can, and one link arrives on the morning of the birthday. It is far easier to fund together than to organise together, and nobody has to guess her taste or her size.

She is not comfortable with computers. Is this the wrong gift?

No. There is nothing to install, nothing to log into and no membership. It opens like a photograph, on whatever screen is in front of her. Somebody sits with her the first time, and after that she can go back to it alone as often as she likes.

We have no written family history. Can you still make one?

Yes. Most families start with less than they think they need. A folder of documents, photographs with something written on the back, and an afternoon of what she still remembers is a real beginning. The research is our part, not yours.

What if she corrects it, or the family remembers more afterwards?

That is what usually happens, and it is planned for. One update round in the first year is included in every full map, so everything the family sends in that year goes in at no extra cost.
