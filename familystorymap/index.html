What the family sees

A century of journeys, in the language each of them reads.

Everyone reads it in their own language. One link opens in Hebrew, Russian or English, and each reader picks their own. Stories, names, tree and every button switch together. Shown with the family’s permission.

Open a place and the story is waiting. The archive photograph, who in the family is connected to it, the passage from the memoir in the writer’s own words, and a line on what stands there today. Nothing paraphrased, nothing invented.

You also get a family tree, interconnected with the map. Click any person to reveal their journey and their stories.

And it prints as a book. Any selection of places exports to a PDF: the photograph, the story in the family’s own words, and a QR code that opens the location. Print it at home, or send it to a relative to review.

What this actually is

We are storytellers, not a genealogy service.

We make your family story come alive on a map, with pictures, memories and geographical pins. We bring relatives together, from across the world and across generations, to visualize, read and relive their history.

It is a family journey map, a keepsake interactive album and a family tree, all interconnected.

Who it’s for

For families holding a story that deserves better than a folder.

A milestone worth marking

A big birthday, an anniversary, a wedding, a reunion. One link the whole family opens on the day, and keeps long after it.

You have already done the research

The memoir is written, the tree is built, the folders are full. This is the part that makes the rest of the family read it.

Ask the one who still remembers

The dates are already on paper. What no archive holds is who your grandmother was at twenty, why she said yes to him, and what she was brave enough to do next. Ask her, and it goes on the map in her own voice.

Private by default

A family story is not a publication. Every map ships on an unlisted link that search engines cannot find, and you decide who gets it. If you want it tighter than that, say so:

  • An unlisted link. Not indexed, not searchable, shared only by you.
  • A password. One word the family shares, and nobody else can open the map.
  • A named list. Access limited to the people you name.

We never publish a family’s map, quote it, or show it as an example without written permission. If you would rather it were public, that is your decision, not ours.
