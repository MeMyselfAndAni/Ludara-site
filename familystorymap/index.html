A Family Story Map

A hundred years of your family,
on one map that brings everyone together.

Most family histories end up as a document nobody opens: a memoir in a drawer, a tree of names and dates, a box of photographs whose places nobody can any longer point to. We turn that material into a living interactive map: a pin for every town, street and house the family lived in, fled from, married in or is buried in, each with the photograph and the story that belongs to it. The whole journey can be drawn as one path across generations, and the family tree is linked to the map, so tapping a name traces exactly where that person’s life took them. In every language your family reads, on any phone, with no app to download.

What the family sees

A century of journeys, in the language each of them reads.

The whole journey as one path. One tap numbers the stops in the order they happened, from Belarus and Lithuania through Russia and Ukraine down to Israel. The regions along the bottom filter the map to one chapter at a time.

Open a place and the story is waiting. The archive photograph, who in the family is connected to it (tap the name and the tree opens on that person), the passage from the memoir in the writer’s own words, and a line on what stands there today. Nothing paraphrased, nothing invented. Every place is listed down the side with its own portrait, branch and dates, and any of them can be bookmarked into a personal selection.

A family tree wired into the map. Generations of three branches, every name carried in all three languages. Click a person and their journey draws itself on the map: the pin on each card is the link between the two.

And it prints as a book. Any selection of places exports to a PDF laid out place by place: the photograph, the story in the family’s own words, and a QR code that opens the location on a map. The file is yours: print it at home, take it to a print shop, or simply send it to a relative who will never open a link.

Why a map, and not another document

A family history you can actually walk through.

It brings the family back together

A book goes to one person and stays on their shelf. A map goes to everyone at once, on a single link, each of them reading it in the language they think in. Relatives who live continents apart find themselves on the same street, on the same evening, looking at the same house.

On the first map we built, family in Belarus and family in Israel are exploring it at the same time, in their own languages.

A pin for every place

The house, the shtetl, the factory, the camp, the first apartment in a new country. We find the historic address, place it accurately, and attach the photograph and the passage of the story that belongs there.

The journey as one path

A century of displacement is hard to hold in your head and obvious on a map. One tap draws the whole route, or one person’s route, in the order it actually happened.

Your own archive, not stock photos

The photographs are yours. Where a family photograph does not exist, we use properly licensed historic or public-domain images of the place, and we credit every one.

Every generation’s language

Grandparents read it in the language they grew up in, grandchildren in the one they were born into. The same link adapts to the reader: we produce every language.

The tree, connected to the map

Not a separate diagram. Tap a person on the family tree and the map draws their life; tap a place and see who was there.

Nothing invented

Every place and every quotation comes from the family’s own material and verified sources. Where the record is uncertain, the map says so rather than filling the gap.
